title: Settings

Settings holds the banks you have connected, plus the financial profile you configure by hand. The budgeting sections work on a fresh account with no bank account connected.

## Bank accounts

This section lists every bank the provider offers for your country, searchable, with the ones you have already connected at the top — each showing how many accounts it brought in and when it last synced. Choosing a bank sends you to it to authorize the access, and it counts as connected only after the bank sends you back.

Every prompt elsewhere in freenary that asks you to connect a bank links straight to this section.

**Disconnect**, on a connected bank's row, removes that bank's accounts with the transactions imported from them, and asks the bank to revoke freenary's access. The confirmation says how many accounts go. Your category corrections survive, and reconnecting imports the transactions again.

## Budgeting profile

A budgeting profile is what you _plan_ each month, declared in three ordered groups: **revenues**, then **investments**, then **outgoings**. Each line carries a name, a monthly amount, and a category.

A Sankey diagram above the form updates as you type, reading left to right:

**revenues → category group → line**

The middle column groups your lines by the _group_ the category you picked belongs to, so a Rent line and a Home insurance line meet in one **Housing** band that then splits into its lines. Investment groups are drawn before outgoing ones, matching the order you enter them.

Revenue you do not allocate appears as a grey **Money left** band, and the strip under the diagram totals revenues, allocated and money left. Allocate more than you earn and that strip flips to **Over-allocated** — the plan is still saved; nothing is clamped.

Amounts are entered in major units (`500` means €500) and saved as whole cents. Saving replaces the whole profile at once, so reordering or removing a line takes effect on save, not before.

## Categories

Categories are organized in two levels. The sixteen **groups** — Income, Investments, Housing, Utilities, Daily living, Transport, Travel, Leisure, Shopping, Subscriptions, Health, Education, Financial, Taxes, Transfers and Other — are fixed, and each opens to reveal the categories inside it. A budget line is assigned a category, never one of these sixteen groups: a group is the heading the category sits under.

Alongside the built-in categories are your own. Create one from the Categories section, or straight from a budget line's category picker when none of the existing ones fit. A custom category has a name unique to your account, a color drawn from the chart palette, an icon, and optionally a group to nest under; nested categories are indented beneath their group and can be reordered within it. Leave the group empty and your category stands on its own as a group — one you can still assign to a budget line, because it holds no categories to pick instead.

Custom categories can be renamed, recolored, re-iconed, re-grouped, reordered and deleted. Each row shows how many budget lines currently use it.

Deleting one never orphans a budget line. Before the category goes away, every line using it is moved to its group's catch-all category — deleting a custom category nested under Housing moves its lines to **Other housing** — or to **Uncategorised** when it belongs to no group. The confirmation tells you exactly how many lines will move and where.

<Callout type="info">
  Custom categories apply to your budgeting profile. Categorizing an imported
  transaction still uses the built-in categories only.
</Callout>
