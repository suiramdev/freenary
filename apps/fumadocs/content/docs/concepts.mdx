Each word below keeps the same meaning on every page of this website, and in the interface.

## Your instance

- **Instance** — one installed copy of Freenary, with its own users and its own database. Read [Self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting).
- **Operator** — the person who runs an instance and picks its options. Read [Configuration](/docs/self-hosting/configuration).

## Your account

- **Account** — your own way into an instance. It holds your name, your email address and every way you sign in. Read [Signing in](/docs/guides/signing-in).
- **One-time code** — a 6-digit code that Freenary emails you. It confirms an address or resets a password, and it lives 10 minutes. Other tools call it an OTP. Read [Signing in](/docs/guides/signing-in).
- **Passkey** — a key your device keeps. Your device unlock signs you in, and you type no password. Read [Signing in](/docs/guides/signing-in).
- **Two-factor authentication** — a code from your authenticator app, on top of your password. Recovery codes stand in when you lose the app. Read [Settings](/docs/guides/settings).
- **Single sign-on** — sign-in through the identity provider that your operator connected. The button reads `Continue with single sign-on`, or the name the operator gave it. Read [Signing in](/docs/guides/signing-in).

## Your banks

- **Bank connection** — one link between Freenary and one of your banks. It holds the accounts that bank shared. Read [Bank connections](/docs/guides/bank-connections).
- **Bank account** — one account inside a bank connection, such as a current account, a savings account or a card. Read [Bank connections](/docs/guides/bank-connections).
- **Sync** — the read that brings new transactions and balances from your bank into Freenary. Read [Bank connections](/docs/guides/bank-connections).
- **Transaction** — one entry your bank booked on an account. It carries a date, an amount and the text the bank sent. Read [Budget](/docs/guides/budget).
- **Company** — the other side of a transaction, as Freenary reads it from that text. The Budget filter named `Company` lists the companies of the period. Read [Budget](/docs/guides/budget).
- **Internal transfer** — a pair of transactions that moves money between two of your own accounts. Freenary matches the pair and files both under `Internal transfer`. Read [Budget](/docs/guides/budget).

## Your budget

- **Category** — the label one transaction carries, such as `Groceries`. Read [Categories](/docs/guides/categories).
- **Category group** — the wider family a category sits in, such as `Housing`. The 16 groups are fixed. Read [Categories](/docs/guides/categories).
- **Custom category** — a category you create, with your own name, colour and icon, under a group of your choice. Read [Categories](/docs/guides/categories).
- **Budget line** — one monthly amount you plan, on one category. Read [Categories](/docs/guides/categories).
- **Budgeting profile** — all your budget lines together. Its side of the flow comes from the group of each line. Read [Categories](/docs/guides/categories).
- **Period** — the date range the Budget screen shows: one month, three months or one year. Read [Budget](/docs/guides/budget).
- **Aggregation** — how a period of many months reports a figure: `Total`, `Monthly avg.` or `Monthly median`. Read [Budget](/docs/guides/budget).
- **Fixed and variable spending** — the two rows of the `Fixed vs. variable` chart. `Fixed` is the spending that repeats; `Variable` is the rest. Read [Budget](/docs/guides/budget).
- **Uncategorised** — the category a transaction keeps while no rule, no dictionary entry and no model decide one for it. Read [Categories](/docs/guides/categories).

## The assistant

- **Assistant** — the part of Home that answers a question about your money from your own data. Each question also reaches the model endpoint the operator configured. Read [The assistant](/docs/guides/assistant).
- **Conversation** — one thread of questions and answers with the assistant. The `New conversation` action files the current thread away. Read [The assistant](/docs/guides/assistant).

## Money

A transaction carries the currency its bank booked it in. The figures of the period and the two totals of the transaction list are in euros.
