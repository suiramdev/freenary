Freenary supports multiple financial scopes rather than assuming one application account represents one isolated individual.

## Personal and household scopes

A personal profile represents your own assets, liabilities and transactions. Household functionality lets partners or family members combine selected financial information while preserving **ownership attribution**: a jointly owned property carries ownership percentages, while personal brokerage accounts stay individually attributed. The application switches freely between an individual position and the combined household position.

## Beyond individuals

The same ownership model can represent companies or holding structures. A user owning part of a company connects or enters company-level assets and sees their indirect economic exposure based on ownership percentage — making Freenary useful for structures conventional personal-finance apps fail to represent correctly.

## Investor profile

A broader financial profile stores information that cannot be inferred from connected accounts: age, household composition, employment situation, investment horizon, objectives, liquidity requirements and risk tolerance. This contextualizes analysis — the same portfolio may suit one person and not another depending on objectives, horizon and cash needs.

The profile is not a one-time onboarding questionnaire: it is part of the financial model and evolves as the user's situation changes.
