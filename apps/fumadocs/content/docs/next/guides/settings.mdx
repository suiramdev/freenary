Settings holds three groups and seven sections. This page walks them in the order the screen uses, and names what each control changes.

Open it from the sidebar. A page that fails to load reads `Could not load your settings`, with a `Try again` button.

| Group | Sections, in order |
| --- | --- |
| `Security` | `Two-factor authentication`, `Passkeys`, `Active sessions`, `Connected accounts` |
| `Connections` | `Bank accounts` |
| `Budgeting` | `Budgeting profile`, `Categories` |

## Security

The group line reads `How you sign in, where your sessions are open, and what protects your account if your password leaks.`

### Two-factor authentication

The section line reads `A code from your authenticator app, on top of your password.` A badge beside the title reads `On` or `Off`.

| State | The body reads |
| --- | --- |
| `Off` | `Your password alone signs you in. Add an authenticator app so a stolen password is not enough.` |
| `On` | `Signing in with your password also asks for a code from your authenticator app.` |

Under it sits the scope note: `A second factor applies to password sign-in. A passkey or a connected provider signs you in on its own.`

| Control | When it appears | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Enable` | The state is `Off` and your account holds a password | Opens the enrolment drawer |
| `Disable` | The state is `On` | Opens a dialog that asks for your password |
| `Generate new recovery codes` | The state is `On` | Opens the drawer at its last stage |

Three dialogs sit behind those controls:

- `Enable` opens `Enable two-factor authentication`. The drawer holds four stages: your password, the QR code, an app code, then the recovery codes.
- `Generate new recovery codes` opens the same drawer as `New recovery codes`, with two stages: your password, then the codes.
- `Disable` opens `Disable two-factor authentication?`, a dialog that asks for your password.

See [Signing in](/docs/guides/signing-in#turn-it-on) for the walkthrough.

You read `Two-factor authentication is on` or `Two-factor authentication is off`.

<Callout type="warn">

An account with no password shows no `Enable` button. It reads `This account has no password — it was created with a connected provider — so a second factor has nothing to guard. A passkey works without one: add it below.` A check that fails instead reads `We could not check whether this account has a password, so two-factor authentication is unavailable for now. Reload to try again.`

</Callout>

### Passkeys

The section line reads `Sign in with your device's own unlock instead of typing a password.`

| Control         | What it does                                       |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `Add a passkey` | Names the passkey, then hands over to your browser |
| `Rename`        | Changes the name alone                             |
| `Remove`        | Stops that passkey from signing you in             |

The naming dialog is titled `Name this passkey`, with the field `Name`, the placeholder `Work laptop` and the default name `This device`. A name holds 60 characters at most, and a longer one reads `Keep the name under {max} characters.` `Rename this passkey` reads `Only the name changes. The passkey itself keeps working.`

Each row names the passkey, or `Unnamed passkey`, then `Added {date}`, then one badge:

| Badge              | What it means                                 |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| `Synced`           | The passkey follows you to your other devices |
| `This device only` | It lives on this device alone                 |
| `Not backed up`    | Your device holds no copy of it elsewhere     |

A removal confirms with `Remove {passkey}?`, over `This device will no longer sign you in, and the passkey stored on it stops working. Your other ways in keep working.`

A browser that cannot create a passkey hides the `Add a passkey` button. It reads `This browser cannot create passkeys here. They need a recent browser on a secure (HTTPS) connection.` With no passkey on the account, that note and the empty explanation appear together.

A list that fails to load reads `Could not load your passkeys`.

### Active sessions

The section line reads `Every browser signed in to your account right now.` A session lasts **7 days**.

Freenary lists your current session first, with the badge `This device`. Each row names the device, then up to three details: `Signed in {date}`, `Last refreshed {date}` and `IP {ip}`. The address line appears only when the session carries one.

Freenary names a device from what the browser reports: `Android device`, `Chromebook`, `iPad`, `iPhone`, `Linux computer`, `Mac`, `Windows PC`, or `Unknown device`.

| Control | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Revoke` | On any row but the current one | Signs that one browser out |
| `Sign out other sessions` | In the header, and only while another session exists | Opens `Sign out other sessions?`, confirmed with `Sign them out` |

A list that fails to load reads `Could not load your active sessions`. The literal empty text is `No active sessions`.

### Connected accounts

The section line reads `The ways you can sign in to this account.`

It lists a `Password` row with `Added {date}` when your account holds one. Then it lists one row per provider your instance offers: `Google`, `Apple` or `Single sign-on`. That last row carries the name your operator gave it.

| Element | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `Connected` | The provider already signs you in |
| `Connect` | Links that provider to this account |
| `Disconnect` | Opens `Disconnect {provider}?`, over `You will no longer be able to sign in with it. Your other ways in keep working.` |
| `This instance no longer offers this provider` | Your operator dropped a provider you had linked |

An account with no password reads `No password is set on this account. Passkeys are not listed here either — they have their own section above.`

A provider list that fails to load reads `Could not load the providers this instance offers, so none can be connected from here`. A failure on your own methods reads `Could not load your sign-in methods`.

<Callout type="warn">

Two refusals arrive as a message:

- `This is the only way into your account. Add another one before removing it.` — Freenary keeps you from a lock-out.
- `Sign in again before changing how your account is accessed: this needs a session less than 15 minutes old.` — it covers a link, an unlink, and a passkey you add or remove.

</Callout>

## Connections

The group line reads `The institutions freenary syncs your accounts from.`

### Bank accounts

One searchable bank list, with your connections at the top. Connect a bank, re-sync one, or disconnect one from here. The whole flow lives on its own page: [Bank connections](/docs/guides/bank-connections).

With no banking service on the instance, the section reads `Bank linking is unavailable` and offers no list.

## Budgeting

The group line reads `The monthly plan you declare by hand, and the categories it is organized with.`

### Budgeting profile

The monthly plan: one amount per category, each with an optional name. Freenary draws it as a live flow diagram above the editor, with a summary and an unsaved-changes bar. See [Categories and budget lines](/docs/guides/categories#the-budgeting-profile).

### Categories

The category list: 16 groups, each a closed row with a count. A built-in category carries the badge `Built-in` and offers no action. Your own categories offer edit, re-parent, reorder and delete, and `New category` sits in the header. See [Categories and budget lines](/docs/guides/categories#custom-categories).

## What Settings does not hold

| You cannot change here | Where it lives instead |
| --- | --- |
| Your password | The reset path on the sign-in screen. See [Signing in](/docs/guides/signing-in#password-reset) |
| Your language | The sidebar account menu. See [Language and appearance](/docs/guides/interface) |
| The appearance | The sidebar account menu. See [Language and appearance](/docs/guides/interface) |

Freenary holds no screen at all for the rest:

- Your display name, your email address and your avatar.
- Account deletion, and any export of your data.
- A notification choice, a currency choice and a date format choice.

Your name and your email come from the account you signed up with.
