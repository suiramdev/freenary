Budget holds two pages. `Transactions` answers where your money came from and where it went, over a period you pick. `Recurring` answers what keeps costing you money, and what it costs you next. This page walks both from top to bottom.

**Before you start:** you need one bank connection. See [Bank connections](/docs/guides/bank-connections).

## With no bank account

The screen holds one card: `Connect a bank account`, over `Connect a bank account to see where your money comes from and where it goes. Your transactions will appear here automatically.` The button `Connect a bank` opens Settings on the bank list.

Nothing else renders. No period controls, no figures, no charts and no list sit behind that card.

## The two pages

The sidebar carries both pages under `Budget`. Press `Budget` to open the pair, and press it again to fold them away.

| Page | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| `Transactions` | Where your money came from, and where it went, over a period you pick. This is the page `Budget` opens |
| `Recurring` | What keeps costing you money, and what it costs you next |

The breadcrumb of the header names both: `Budget`, then the page you are on. With the sidebar collapsed to its icons, the `Budget` icon opens `Transactions` on its own.

The period row belongs to `Transactions` alone. The `1M` `3M` `1Y` range, the period button, the two arrows and the aggregation menu all go with it. `Recurring` reads a fixed trailing 12 months instead, and it states that in its first line.

<Callout type="info">

The whole view sits in the address of the page. The period, every chart choice, the sort and every filter are all in it. Copy the address and you share exactly what you see. A view at its default value leaves the address alone. A filter you change does not fill your Back button.

</Callout>

## The Transactions page

### The period

The first row of the page sets the period.

| Control | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| Range | Three buttons: `1M`, `3M` and `1Y`. `1M` is the default |
| Period button | The current period, as in `August 2026`, `Jun – Aug 2026` or `2026` |
| Arrows | One each side. A screen reader reads `Previous period` and `Next period` |
| Aggregation | A menu with no label of its own. Its button shows the current value |

A period always ends on the last day of the anchor month. One month is the `1M` range. The `3M` range adds the two months before it. The `1Y` range adds the eleven before it, so it anchors on December.

An arrow steps one whole range at a time: one month, three months or twelve. The arrow greys out at the first month and at the last month your data covers.

#### Pick a month or a year

Press the period button to open a picker.

- On `1M` and `3M` it opens a calendar, and you pick the anchor month.
- On `1Y` a grid of 12 years takes the place of that calendar. Two arrows page the grid, and a screen reader reads them as `Previous years` and `Next years`.

<Callout type="warn">

The three ranges are the whole choice. No control takes a start date and an end date.

</Callout>

#### Aggregation

The aggregation menu appears on `3M` and `1Y` only, because one month has nothing to average. It carries no visible label, and its button reads the current value.

| Value            | What the figures become                    |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| `Total`          | The sum over the whole period. The default |
| `Monthly avg.`   | The mean month of the period               |
| `Monthly median` | The median month of the period             |

Go back to `1M` and the menu goes away, with the value reset to `Total`.

### The three figures

Three cells sit under the period row, in this order: income, expenses, then the difference. The aggregation you picked renames all three.

| Aggregation      | First cell    | Second cell     | Third cell |
| ---------------- | ------------- | --------------- | ---------- |
| `Total`          | `Income`      | `Expenses`      | `Net`      |
| `Monthly avg.`   | `Avg. Income` | `Avg. Expenses` | `Avg. Net` |
| `Monthly median` | `Med. Income` | `Med. Expenses` | `Med. Net` |

The third cell turns green at zero and above, and red below it.

A cell whose figure fails to load prints a dash, not a zero. A screen reader hears `Unavailable` there. Freenary prefers an empty cell to a number it did not measure.

### The two charts

The page holds exactly two chart positions, and no control adds a third.

| Position | Control | Options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left, wide | A two-button toggle. A screen reader reads it as `Chart view` | `Flow` and `Categories` |
| Right, narrow | A menu. A screen reader reads it as `Companion chart` | `Fixed vs. variable` and `Budget vs. actual` |

The wide card is titled `Cash Flow` on `Flow`, and `Spending Breakdown` on `Categories`. On an aggregation other than `Total`, the title gains ` · Monthly avg.` or ` · Monthly median`. The narrow chart carries no separate title: its own menu button names the view.

Both charts hold a fixed height, so a switch between two views never moves the list under them. A chart that fails to load reads `Could not load this chart.` and draws no figures.

#### Flow

`Flow` is a Sankey diagram with three columns: your income sources, then the category groups, then the categories inside them.

- Press a group node to filter the list to that whole group.
- Press a category node to filter the list to that one category.
- An income node answers no press.
- Income you did not spend forms a grey node labelled `Money left`. It answers no press either.

With nothing on either side, the card reads `No money moved in this period.`

#### Categories

`Categories` is a ring over the **16 category groups**, not over the 75 categories inside them. Sixteen slices carry more than seventy-five would.

Press a slice, or its row in the legend, to filter the list to that group. Press the same one again to clear the filter. The slices you did not pick fade. Point at a slice and a tooltip gives the label, the amount and the share as a percentage.

With nothing spent, the card reads `Nothing spent in this period.`

#### Fixed vs. variable

Two rows, `Fixed` and `Variable`, each with an amount and a percentage of your spending.

This chart is read-only. It answers no press, and it filters nothing. No category maps onto the split between a recurring payment and a one-off. Empty, it reads `Nothing spent in this period.`

#### Budget vs. actual

One row per category group, planned against real, all on one shared scale. The key above the rows reads `Actual` `/` `Planned`.

| A row reads     | It means                                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `{amount} over` | You spent more than the plan for that group |
| `{amount} left` | You spent less than the plan for that group |
| `Unplanned`     | You declared no line in that group          |

Press a row to filter the list to that group.

With no plan at all, the card reads `No spending plan yet.` and offers the button `Set a spending plan`. It opens Settings at the top of the page, so scroll down to `Budgeting profile`. See [Categories and budget lines](/docs/guides/categories#the-budgeting-profile).

With a plan and nothing spent, the card reads `Nothing spent in this period.`

### Search, sort and filter

A toolbar sits above the list.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Search box | Reads `Search transactions…`. It searches the text of a transaction |
| Sort | A screen reader reads it as `Sort`. The options are `Newest` and `Largest` |
| `Amount` | A floor, a ceiling, or both |
| `Company` | A searchable menu over the companies of the period |
| `Category` | A searchable menu over the 16 groups and their categories |

Each trigger carries a count badge while it filters.

#### Amount

The popover holds `Minimum` and `Maximum`. Each field carries the placeholder `Any`, and `Clear` empties both. The same bounds hold on `Outgoing` and on `Incoming`, because the sign plays no part. Under the fields sits the hint `Matches how much money moved, whichever way it went.`

A floor above the ceiling reads `The minimum is above the maximum.` Freenary applies it anyway, so the list goes empty.

#### Company

The menu searches with `Search companies…`. Each row names a company of the period, with the total for it. The most frequent company comes first. Where your bank named no company, Freenary lists the description it cleaned up instead.

| State                      | What you read                    |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| The search matched nothing | `No companies match your search` |
| The list failed to load    | `Could not load the companies`   |

#### Category

The menu searches with `Search categories…`, and an empty result reads `No categories match your search`. Each group carries its own checkbox above its categories. Tick a group and Freenary ticks every category inside it, then greys them out: the group already covers them.

#### Chips and Clear all

Every active filter becomes a badge above the list. A screen reader reads its remove button as `Remove {label} filter`.

`Clear all` appears at two active filters or more, and it clears the categories, the companies and the amount. An amount range counts as **one** filter, whether you set one bound or both.

### The transaction list

Two tabs head the list, `Outgoing · {amount}` and `Incoming · {amount}`, each with its own total for the period. The tab you pick decides which side the list shows.

The list groups rows by the range you picked. It groups by day on `1M`, by week on `3M` and by month on `1Y`. On `1M` alone, the first two group headers read `Today` and `Yesterday`.

Rows arrive **50** at a time as you reach the foot of the list. A screen reader reads a row as `{title}, paid {amount}` or `{title}, received {amount}`.

An empty period reads `No transactions`, over `No outgoing transactions found for this period.` or `No incoming transactions found for this period.`

### Fix a category

Press a row to open a drawer titled `Transaction details`. It holds `Date`, then `Description` when your bank gave both a company and a description, then `Category`.

<Steps>

<Step>

#### Open the category picker

The picker searches the **75** built-in categories, grouped under their category group.

</Step>

<Step>

#### Pick the right category

Freenary saves at once. A failure reads `Failed to update category`.

</Step>

<Step>

#### Undo it if you must

A button appears once your choice differs from the one Freenary derived. A screen reader reads it as `Reset to auto-detected category`.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout type="info">

A correction is not local to one transaction. Freenary records the company of that transaction and files every other transaction of the same company under your category. A transaction you already corrected by hand keeps the category you gave it. A later import of that company gets your category too.

</Callout>

<Callout type="warn">

The category is the only field you change here. No screen adds, edits or deletes a transaction, and the picker offers no custom category. See [Categories and budget lines](/docs/guides/categories#custom-categories).

</Callout>

## Sync from this screen

Freenary runs one quiet sync when you open Budget with bank accounts, once per visit. It reports a failure and nothing else.

`Sync transactions` sits above both pages, at the right of the first row. It runs a full re-read for every connection. Every sync reads a window of 90 days at most. The button changes how much of that window Freenary re-reads. It never reaches further back. See [Bank connections](/docs/guides/bank-connections#sync).

## The Recurring page

`Recurring` answers two questions: what keeps costing you money, and what it costs you next. Its first line names the scope: `Detected from your last 12 months of transactions.`

### Insights

A strip of at most four short lines opens the page. Freenary writes a line only for what it measured, and with nothing measurable the strip does not render at all.

- `{count} recurring payments are due in the next 7 days.`
- `Recurring costs rose {percent} over the last 3 months.`, or `fell`, or `Recurring costs held steady over the last 3 months.`
- `Recurring costs use {percent} of your monthly plan.`, or `… of your monthly income.`
- `{company} costs {amount} per year.`, for the dearest commitment.

The trend line compares the last three whole months against the three before them. It leaves the month in progress out. A move under 2 percent reads as steady.

### The six figures

Six cells sit under the insights, in two rows of three.

| Cell | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `Per month` | What every commitment costs in a month |
| `Per year` | The same figure over a year |
| `Share of plan`, or `Share of income` | The monthly cost against your declared plan, or against the income Freenary measured when you declared no plan |
| `Left after recurring` | That same figure minus the monthly cost. It turns green above zero and red below it |
| `Commitments` | How many there are. A second line counts the spending patterns beside them |
| `Due in 30 days` | Payments expected in the next 30 days, of both kinds. A second line adds what they come to |

A cell Freenary did not measure prints a dash, not a zero. `Share of income` and `Left after recurring` print that dash when you have neither a plan nor measured income.

### The charts

The page holds exactly two chart positions, and no control adds a third. Each position holds three views and a fixed height, so a switch moves nothing below it.

The wide position carries a three-button toggle. A screen reader reads it as `Recurring chart`.

| View | Card title | What it draws |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Over time` | `Recurring Over Time` | One stacked bar a month over 12 months: `Commitments`, `Repeated` and `Other` |
| `Forecast` | `Cash-Flow Forecast` | Your balance walked forward 30 days as each expected payment lands |
| `Cost vs. frequency` | `Cost vs. Frequency` | One point per company: payments a year against yearly cost |

On `Forecast`, the first point reads `Today`. A dashed red line marks zero when the balance runs out. With no balance reported by your bank, the card reads `No balance reported, so no forecast.`

On `Cost vs. frequency`, blue marks a commitment and orange marks a repeated purchase. The other charts of the page use those same two colours.

The narrow position carries a menu. A screen reader reads it as `Companion chart`.

| View | What it draws |
| --- | --- |
| `By category` | The eight dearest categories a month, on one shared scale |
| `Most frequent` | Companies by how often Freenary saw them, with the count a year and the yearly cost |
| `Recurring vs. other` | One bar split three ways, with an amount and a share for each |

`By category` is read-only. It answers no press, and it filters nothing.

### Filter and sort

A toolbar sits above the list, with the same controls the transaction list carries.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Search box | Reads `Search companies…`. It searches the company of a recurrence |
| Sort | A screen reader reads it as `Sort`. The options are `Dearest` and `Soonest` |
| `Cost` | A floor, a ceiling, or both, on what a payment costs a month |
| `Recurrence` | A menu over the five frequencies and the three confidence levels |
| `Category` | A searchable menu over the 16 groups and their categories |

Each trigger carries a count badge while it filters. Every active filter becomes a badge above the list, and `Clear all` appears at two active filters or more. A cost range counts as **one** filter, whether you set one bound or both.

Under the fields of `Cost` sits a hint: `Matches what a payment costs a month, whatever its cadence.` A yearly premium of 240 answers a floor of 20. It does not answer a floor of 240.

No filter asks your server for anything. Freenary reads the year once, so a filter narrows the rows and both totals at once.

### The recurring list

Two tabs head the list: `Commitments · {amount}` and `Patterns · {amount}`. Each amount is what that side costs a month, under the filters you set. The tab you pick decides which side shows. A line under the tabs says what that side holds.

| Tab | Its line |
| --- | --- |
| `Commitments` | `Payments on a regular cadence for a steady amount.` |
| `Patterns` | `Read from repeated purchases, not from a confirmed commitment.` |

The columns come in this order: `Company`, `Confidence`, `Amount`, `Frequency`, `Next`, `Per month` and `Per year`. The `Company` column carries the category under the name. An amount that moves from one payment to the next reads `≈ {amount}`. The `Next` column carries the calendar date. Under that date sits the relative day for a date inside 30 days: `Today`, `Tomorrow` or `In {n} days`.

A narrow panel drops `Per year` first, then `Per month`, then `Frequency`. `Company`, `Confidence`, `Amount` and `Next` always show.

A side with nothing in it keeps its name over a line of its own. `Commitments` reads `No commitment detected.`, and `Spending patterns` reads `No spending pattern detected.` A filter that matches nothing reads `No match` over `No recurring payment matches these filters.`

### Commitments and patterns

The two tabs of the list hold the two kinds of recurrence.

| Tab | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `Commitments` | The company repeats on a regular cadence for an amount that holds: a subscription, rent, an insurance premium, a phone bill |
| `Patterns` | The company repeats, and the amount does not: groceries, coffee, transport, online shopping |

`Confidence` holds one of three values.

| Confidence | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `Confirmed` | Same company, same cadence, same amount, and seen three times or more |
| `Likely` | Same company on a regular cadence, with an amount that moves, or seen only twice so far |
| `Pattern` | A repeated purchase, and not a confirmed commitment |

<Callout type="info">

Freenary asks for neither an exact date nor an exact amount. A bank moves its processing date, and a utility bill moves its amount. [Recurrence detection](/docs/contributing/categorisation#recurrence-detection) holds how Freenary reads a cadence out of your transactions.

</Callout>

### With nothing detected

The page keeps its first line and shows one card: `No recurring payment detected yet`, over `Freenary needs a few months of transactions from the same company before it can read a cadence.`

No figures, no charts and no tables sit behind that card.
