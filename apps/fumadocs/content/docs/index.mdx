Freenary is an open-source personal finance and wealth-management platform inspired by applications such as Finary, but aiming to go significantly further in openness, extensibility, financial coverage and artificial intelligence.

It is not a clone with a different interface. Freenary reproduces the strongest ideas behind modern wealth-management applications and transforms them into an open financial platform that users can host, extend, customize and connect to the financial services of their choice.

## A personal financial operating system

Instead of managing your financial life across separate banking apps, brokerage platforms, cryptocurrency exchanges, budgeting apps, spreadsheets and calculators, Freenary brings these sources together into one coherent environment. Bank accounts, savings accounts, investment portfolios, cryptocurrencies, real estate, loans, private assets, recurring expenses, income and financial goals all become part of the same financial model.

The project can be summarized through three questions:

- **What** — a unified open-source platform for managing, understanding and improving an individual's or household's finances.
- **How** — aggregation from banking APIs and other providers, manual asset management, normalized transactions and holdings, portfolio analytics, budgeting, financial planning, and an AI layer that reasons across all of it.
- **Why** — a clear understanding of your complete financial position, plus useful assistance for virtually any financial question or decision.

## More than a portfolio tracker

A portfolio tracker answers one question: "What are my investments worth?" Freenary is designed to also answer:

- Where is my money going?
- How much can I safely spend?
- What subscriptions could I cancel?
- Am I investing enough? How diversified am I?
- Can I afford this purchase?
- When could I reach financial independence?
- How would buying a property affect my finances? What happens if my income falls?

## The progression

Connect the financial data → understand the present → analyze what can be improved → define where you want to go → simulate possible futures → make better-informed decisions.
