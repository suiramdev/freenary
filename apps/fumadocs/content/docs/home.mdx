The Home section answers the simplest and most important question: **"What is my situation right now?"** It is the entry point into Freenary: rather than inspecting individual bank and investment accounts, you ask.

## Ask the assistant

Home is a conversation with Freenary. Ask "where did my money go last month?" or "which subscriptions am I paying for?", and the assistant looks the answer up in your own accounts and transactions before writing it. The piggy bank beside each answer shows what it is doing while it works.

One conversation is kept, and it survives a reload; **New conversation** sets the old thread aside and starts again. What it can look up, and what it deliberately cannot do yet, is documented under [AI](/docs/ai).

## First steps

Until your setup is complete, the sidebar carries a **First steps** checklist with the three things worth doing before any area has data to show:

- **Connect your bank** — opens the [Bank accounts](/docs/settings#bank-accounts) section of Settings.
- **Set up your budget** — opens the [Budgeting profile](/docs/settings#budgeting-profile) section of Settings.
- **Protect your account** — opens the [Security](/docs/signing-in) group of Settings, and is ticked by either an authenticator app or a passkey.

Each item is ticked from your own records — a connected bank, a saved budget line — rather than from whether you walked through the onboarding steps, so skipping them leaves the checklist correct. There is nothing to dismiss: it disappears on its own once all three are done, and comes back if you disconnect your last bank.

## Wealth indicator

A prominent wealth indicator displays gross wealth, net worth or financial assets:

- **Gross wealth** — total value of assets before liabilities are deducted.
- **Net worth** — assets minus debts and other liabilities.
- **Financial assets** — a narrower view focused on liquid and investable wealth.

Moving between these perspectives matters because property value, available investment capital and true net position can be very different numbers.

## Historical chart

A historical wealth chart shows how your financial position evolves over days, months and years. Freenary distinguishes changes created by **market performance** from changes created by **user cash flows**: depositing €10,000 into an investment account increases its value but does not represent €10,000 of profit. Separating contributions, withdrawals and real performance makes the data considerably more meaningful.

## Other indicators

Home can surface monthly income, monthly spending, savings rate, available cash, debt, investment performance and goal progress. Rather than displaying every possible metric, it acts as a control center: identifying what changed, highlighting what may need attention, and offering direct paths into detailed sections.
